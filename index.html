Association is a Trap

Most hiring mistakes happen because we value "shared associations" more than actual performance evidence.

The Interview Impact: We often confuse "having talent" with "sharing my associations." If you like a candidate because they also drink black coffee or play soccer, your brain is flagging them as "safe" instead of "skilled."

The Danger Zone

The Gut Trap

Research identifies these common styles as the primary causes of mis-hires. Which one is your "Brain Noise"?

The Art Critic

Trusts their "gut" in the first 10 minutes. This is just Affinity Bias masquerading as expertise. You are hiring someone you'd like to have a beer with, not necessarily an A-player.

The Sponge

Lets everyone interview but has no specific plan. This creates Noise because every interviewer is looking for a different "vibe" instead of a specific skill.

The Chatterbox

Spends 45 minutes talking about how great Gravity is. You end the call liking the candidate simply because they were a good listener, not a good worker.

The Efficiency Gap

The Cognitive Miser

Human brains are biologically programmed to find the path of least resistance.

When faced with millions of bits of data per second, your brain relies on "System 1"—the fast, automatic, and emotional engine that skips the hard work of analysis to give you an instant "feeling."

"System 1 operates automatically and quickly, with little or no effort and no sense of voluntary control."

— Daniel Kahneman

Workshop: The Stroop Test

Say the COLOR of the text, not the word itself, as fast as possible:

BLUE

Discussion: Your System 1 read "BLUE" instantly. Your System 2 had to fight to identify the color. Interviewing is the same—ignoring "charm" to focus on "skill" takes cognitive energy.

The Brain Bus

The Decision Dashboard

Let's look at the hardware that's actually running your interviews.

The Amygdala

"The Alarm System"

It scans for people your brain flags as "not like us." It sees Different and flags it as Danger.

How this blocks you: You feel "tight" or "uncomfortable" on a call. You write it off as a bad vibe, but it's just your brain resisting difference.

The Hippocampus

"The Pattern Library"

It stores societal tropes and associations. It tells you: "Successful people usually look/talk like THIS."

How this blocks you: You start looking for evidence to prove your first 30-second association right, ignoring all other talent data.

Prefrontal Cortex

"The Pilot"

The only part that can read a Scorecard. It uses logic to override the "Gut." It's high-energy and gets tired easily.

How this fuels you: This is where objectivity lives. We use Competencies to keep the Pilot in control of the decision.

Debrief Room Traps

Recognizing "Brain Noise" in real Gravity huddles.

"The Mesh Fallacy"

"I don't think they'd mesh well with the team."

The Real Issue: Code for Affinity Bias. We want Culture Add. If everyone meshes perfectly, we stop challenging each other's ideas.

"The Passion Trap"

"They didn't seem excited about our mission."

The Real Issue: You're rewarding extroversion. Look for Preparation (research) rather than emotional energy levels.

"The Decision For Them"

"They've managed before, they'll get bored here."

The Real Issue: Assumption about their ego. If they have the skills, let them decide if they'll be bored.

"The Pedigree Shortcut"

"They worked at [Big Bank], they won't be scrappy."

The Real Issue: Judging the past employer logo. Test for Grit directly instead of assuming.

Gravity Audit

Contextual Barriers

Are you testing for Ability or Access?

Gravity Example 1

"Explain how interchange fees affect a merchant's bottom line."

The Trap: Industry access test. Unless senior payments role, this identifies if they've worked for a competitor.

Better Question: "Tell me about a complex system you had to learn quickly." (Tests for Learning Speed).

Gravity Example 2

"Tell me about a time you handled a difficult 5-star customer."

The Trap: Assumes "5-star" background. A diner server might have more Grit than a server at a luxury hotel.

Better Question: "Tell me about a time you had to keep your cool while a customer was being unreasonable." (Tests for Composure).
