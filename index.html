Debrief Room Traps

Common language that masks underlying bias.

✕
Root: Fortune Teller

"They'll get bored"

"I don't think they'd be happy in this role long-term. They've managed teams before, so they'll get bored."

The Reality: Rejection based on an assumption about their desires. Hire for role capability, not hypothetical boredom.

✕
Root: Art Critic

"The Mesh Fallacy"

"I just don't think they would mesh well with the team."

The Reality: "Meshing" often masks affinity bias (hiring for sameness). We want Culture Add, not Culture Replication.

✕
Root: Pedigree Bias

"The Startup Filter"

"They haven't worked in a startup, so they won't handle the pace."

The Reality: Judging environment over skill. Logo-based assumptions ignore individual agility and troubleshooting logic.

The Exposure Audit

Contextual Barriers

How our questions accidentally test for privilege rather than actual capability.

Manager: Steve Goal: Technical troubleshooting

"Explain how to program a multi-zone smart thermostat."

Trap: Wealth & Exposure. Steve wants to see troubleshooting logic, but a candidate in rentals may lack this specific exposure while being an expert problem-solver.

Manager: Sarah Goal: Service Standards

"Describe a time you received 5-star service at fine-dining."

Trap: Socioeconomic Background. Sarah wants to assess service intuition, but a candidate may lack fine-dining experience while possessing elite communication skills.

Competency Brake Pedal

Rely on Facts

Competencies are the specific "Brake Pedals" that force the CEO brain to take over. We stop evaluating "vibes" and start evaluating **measurable skill**.

  • Curiosity: Seeking the root cause before acting. Questioning assumptions.

  • Communication: Articulating complex ideas clearly to different audiences.

  • Drives Results: Focus on outcomes and persistence rather than just busyness.

  • Collaborates: Elevating the work of those around them and building consensus.

The Evidence Rule

Feelings are automatic associations. Every rating requires factual behavior observed or a direct quote.

If you can't find factual proof in your notes, don't give the score.
