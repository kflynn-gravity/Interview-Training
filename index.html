Debrief Room Traps

How bias translates into common hiring language.

✕
Root: Fortune Teller

"They'll get bored"

"I don't think they'd be happy in this role long-term. They've managed teams before, so they'll get bored."

The Reality: You are rejecting them based on an assumption about their desires. We hire for role capability. Let the candidate decide what they want.

✕
Root: Art Critic

"The Mesh Fallacy"

"I just don't think they would mesh well with the team."

The Reality: "Meshing" is often a veil for affinity bias (hiring for sameness). We want Culture Add, not Culture Replication.

✕
Root: Pedigree Bias

"The Startup Filter"

"They haven't worked in a startup, so they won't handle the structure / handle the pace."

The Reality: You are judging their environment, not their skill. Large-company backgrounds don't guarantee rigidity, nor do startups guarantee agility. Test for the skill, not the logo.

The Exposure Audit

Contextual Barriers

How our questions accidentally test for privilege rather than actual capability.

Context: Residential

"Explain how to program a multi-zone smart thermostat."

Trap: Tests for Wealth & Exposure. Someone living in rentals may lack this specific context, while possessing superior troubleshooting logic.

Context: Consumer

"Describe a time you received 5-star service at fine-dining."

Trap: Tests for Socioeconomic Background. A service expert may lack a fine-dining reference point, but be an expert at communication.

Competency Brake Pedal

Rely on Facts

Competencies are the specific "Brake Pedal" that forces the CEO brain to take over. We stop evaluating "vibes" and start evaluating **measurable skill**.

  • Curiosity: Seeking the root cause before acting. Questioning assumptions.

  • Communication: Articulating complex ideas clearly to different audiences.

  • Drives Results: Focus on outcomes and persistence rather than just busyness.

  • Collaborates: Elevating the work of those around them and building consensus.

The Evidence Rule

Feelings are just automatic associations trying to bypass your critical thinking. To hire A-players, every rating must be backed by a direct quote or a specific, factual behavior observed.

If you can't find factual proof in your notes, don't give the score.
